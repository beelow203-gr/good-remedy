Where workflows break

The failure is often bigger than the prompt.

Context and sources

Instructions drift, corrections disappear, or the wrong material becomes current.

Steps and handoffs

Important work is skipped, repeated, or lost when the next person takes over.

Integrations and actions

Tools stop, duplicate work, or move the wrong result into another system.

Review and completion

The output looks finished before the evidence, approval, or real result is there.

The repair engagement

Fix the operating failure, then test it where the work happens.

  1. 1

    Reproduce the failure

    Start with a real example and identify where the operation stops holding together.

  2. 2

    Repair the weak point

    Change the prompt, source, handoff, integration, review step, or operating boundary that is actually causing the problem.

  3. 3

    Test against agreed examples

    Show what improved, what remains open, and what still belongs to a person.

  4. 4

    Stabilize or hand off

    Return the repaired operation with support, or keep it in Good Remedy.

What you receive

  • A repaired operation tested against agreed examples
  • A clear account of the failure and repair
  • Visible remaining dependencies
  • A usable handoff
  • A support option after launch
  • A path into the Good Remedy platform when useful

Common questions

Can you repair work built by someone else?

Yes, when the current tools and access allow it. Good Remedy can also work beside the original builder.

Do you only fix prompts?

No. The prompt may be fine. The failure may be in the sources, handoff, integration, approval, or definition of done.

Do you need production access right away?

No. Start with a sanitized example and the failure people keep seeing.

Will you replace the whole stack?

Not unless the stack is the actual problem. One broken operation does not justify rebuilding everything.
