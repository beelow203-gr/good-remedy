When repair is the right move

The model may be capable. The operation is not carrying the business correctly.

  • Old instructions or obsolete facts return after they were corrected.
  • People rebuild context before useful work can continue.
  • The output looks complete while material pieces are missing.
  • A handoff fails unless the original expert explains the work again.
  • The workflow uses the wrong record, owner, approval, or completion standard.
  • Senior staff remain the real operating and quality control system.

What Good Remedy repairs

Find the break between AI capability and the business operation.

We examine the operation in the setting where the team uses it, identify where business context, authority, continuity, or handoff is failing, and test the revised version against agreed examples.

What you receive

  • A repaired operation tested against agreed examples
  • A clear account of what improved and what remains open
  • The work that still requires a person
  • A usable handoff
  • A support option after repair
  • A path into the Good Remedy platform when useful

Common questions

Can you repair an operation built by another team?

Yes, when the tools and access allow it. Good Remedy can also work beside the original builder or implementation partner.

Do you only fix prompts?

No. The failure may be in the records, authority, handoff, integration, review point, or definition of completion.

Do you need production access right away?

No. Start with a sanitized example and the failure people keep seeing.

Will you replace the whole stack?

Not unless the stack is the actual problem. Repair should begin with the smallest useful change.
