When repair is the right move

The workflow exists, but the operation still depends on manual rescue.

  • Instructions or corrections disappear between runs.
  • People rebuild context before useful work can continue.
  • The output looks complete while material pieces are missing.
  • Handoffs fail unless the original expert explains the work again.
  • Automations stop, duplicate work, or send the wrong result onward.
  • Senior staff remain the real quality control system.

What Good Remedy does

Find the point where the workflow stops being dependable.

We examine the operation in the setting where the team uses it, repair the weak part, and test the revised version against agreed examples.

Common questions

Can you repair a workflow built by another team?

Yes, when the tools and access allow it. Good Remedy can also work beside the original builder or implementation partner.

Do you only fix prompts?

No. The failure may be in the data, handoff, integration, review point, approval, or definition of completion.

Do you need production access right away?

No. Start with a sanitized example and the failure people keep seeing.

Will you replace the whole stack?

Not unless the stack is the actual problem. Most repair work should begin with the smallest useful change.
