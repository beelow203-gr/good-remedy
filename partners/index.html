Where Good Remedy fits

The technology is in place. The operation still has to work.

  • An AI rollout the client has bought but not yet put into daily use.
  • An automation, migration, or platform change that alters how a team works.
  • A deployment that works technically but is not yet trusted or accepted by the people who rely on it.
  • An operation that needs clear approvals, exception handling, and proof of what actually happened.

What Good Remedy contributes

A bounded part of the engagement, defined before work starts.

  • Operating design for one operation: its purpose, inputs, decisions, exceptions, and who may approve what.
  • Acceptance criteria and evidence the client can check.
  • Workflow repair where an existing deployment keeps failing.
  • A Diligence Readout of an important AI assisted deliverable before it reaches the client.
  • Ongoing operational verification, where that is agreed.

What stays with you

Your client, your stack, your methods.

  • The client relationship and commercial lead
  • Infrastructure, security, identity, and integrations
  • The models, tools, and platforms you deploy
  • Your delivery methods and brand with the client

How we can work together

Three arrangements, stated before any work begins.

  • Introduction. You introduce Good Remedy, the client contracts with Good Remedy directly for its part, and you remain the lead on technical delivery.
  • Subcontract. Good Remedy works under your agreement with the client, inside the scope you hold.
  • White label. Not offered as a standard arrangement. If it ever makes sense, it is agreed case by case in writing.

Confidentiality and handoff

Agreed in writing before client material is shared.

Before any client material is shared, the engagement states what is shared, who can access it, how it is used, and when it is returned or deleted. Client material is used only for that engagement. At handoff, you and the client receive the operating design, the acceptance evidence, and the open items in a form your team can keep running.
