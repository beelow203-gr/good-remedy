A real operation, not a branded assistant

The pilot tests whether AI can carry a real piece of business work.

The operation is built around current records, named owners, human decisions, approved tools, and a result the business can judge.

What the pilot includes

A working environment with a clear operating boundary.

  • A defined business outcome
  • Current records and governing inputs
  • Named owners and decision points
  • Approved models, tools, and connections
  • An observed result in real use
  • A recommendation to stop, repair, continue, or expand

A good first candidate

Recurring, important, and currently held together by people.

The strongest first operation happens often, matters enough to review, and still requires experienced people to restore context, check the work, or repair the handoff.

After the pilot

Continue only when the result is useful.

  • Stop if the operation does not produce a useful result.
  • Repair the weak part if the use is promising but the operation still fails.
  • Keep the client environment and support the accepted operation.
  • Expand into additional teams and operations when the business is ready.

Common questions

Is the pilot a chatbot setup?

No. The pilot is one business operation with real records, owners, decisions, tools, and a result.

Do we have to change models or platforms?

No. Good Remedy begins with the tools the business already uses and changes only what the operation requires.

Do you need production access on the first call?

No. Start with a sanitized example. Access and data handling are agreed only after the operating boundary is clear.

What happens if the pilot works?

The client can keep the environment, add support or managed operations, and expand into additional uses.
