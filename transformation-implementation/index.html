What the pilot includes

A real operating test, not a strategy workshop.

  1. 1

    Define the outcome

    Agree on the operation, owner, expected result, and boundary before the work expands.

  2. 2

    Configure the operation

    Connect the approved tools, records, people, and decisions required to run it.

  3. 3

    Run real examples

    Use the operation with the team in the setting where the work actually happens.

  4. 4

    Judge the result

    Review what worked, what did not, what remains human, and whether to continue.

What you receive

  • A configured operation in a client environment
  • A clear client view of work and decisions
  • Results from agreed test examples
  • Open dependencies and human decisions
  • A handoff or support plan
  • A recommendation to stop, repair, continue, or expand

What happens next

Keep only what proves useful.

The company can stop after the pilot, repair the operation, keep it under an ongoing platform agreement, or expand into additional operations.

Pilot data boundary

A sanitized example is enough to begin. Data handling, model and tool use, access, retention, and approved connections are agreed before live work begins. Good Remedy does not ask for production access during the first conversation.

Common questions

Is this consulting or software?

It is a managed software implementation. Good Remedy stays close to the first operation because the operating capability has to work before a broader agreement means anything.

Do we have to change models or platforms?

No. Good Remedy starts with the tools already in place and changes only what the operation requires.

How long does a pilot take?

The scope and timing depend on the operation, systems, and access involved. The boundary is agreed before the work begins.

What should we bring?

The recurring job, one real example, the systems involved, and the result somebody needs.
